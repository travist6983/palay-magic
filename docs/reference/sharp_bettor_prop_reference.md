# The Complete Sharp Bettor's Reference to NFL Props & Markets on FanDuel and DraftKings (2025–2026)

## TL;DR
- **DraftKings offers the deeper prop menu (70–90 player-prop markets per game and more alternate lines on stars), while FanDuel is the liquidity leader on anytime-touchdown and moves TD lines fastest** — but both books settle props off the **official NFL gamebook** (not PFF/team charting), which is the single most important edge-and-trap distinction across the entire board.
- **The softest, most exploitable markets are defensive tackle props (especially every-down off-ball linebackers), secondary-receiver receiving yards/receptions, and alt lines that lag injury news; the biggest traps are anytime/first-TD scorer (6–10% hold), 4+ leg SGPs (15–30% hold), and passing-TD props (juice-only pricing).** Line-shop everything with OddsJam, Unabated, Outlier or OddsShopper.
- **Settlement edge cases decide bets after the football is over:** longest-completion/rush/reception settles Under if the player records none; a QB passing TD does NOT count for anytime-TD; inactive players void the bet (Under still pays if a player leaves injured mid-game); FanDuel counts special-teams tackles in Tackles+Assists while DraftKings excludes them; half-sacks count as 0.5 / "Yes"; full-game props include OT but quarter/half segment props exclude it.

## Key Findings

1. **Both books grade defensive and skill props off the official NFL gamebook, not third-party charting.** Bettors who model tackle props off PFF or team-charted numbers (which run higher) will systematically overestimate settled totals. This is the most common structural mistake in defensive props.

2. **Tackle props are widely cited as the softest NFL prop market.** They are highly predictable (driven by snap share, opponent run rate, and pace), carry lower vig than most props, and books devote little modeling attention to them. The edge concentrates in every-down off-ball linebackers, not safeties or corners (whose usage is noisier and whose lines bundle random INT variance).

3. **DraftKings = breadth and softer niche pricing; FanDuel = polish, TD liquidity, more alt spreads/totals, and the pioneering SGP builder.** Sharps use both and line-shop. DraftKings runs softer on player props and niche markets but limits winning accounts aggressively.

4. **Correlation is the core of SGP value.** FanDuel and DraftKings both price correlation into same-game parlays ("correlation tax"), but inconsistently — the same two-leg build can be priced at, e.g., +290 on one book and +180 on the other. The profitable approach is to compare the SGP price to the fair joint probability, which requires a tool.

5. **Prop lines in 2025–26 are set by automated pricing engines** consuming official league feeds (including NFL Next Gen Stats), with human traders overriding on injury/weather/steam. The remaining human edge is qualitative and contextual — reading beat reporters and usage shifts before the model's baseline catches up.

## Details

### How prop lines are set, and how/why they move
Prop lines are generated and continuously updated by automated pricing engines that pull official league play-by-play, box scores, and NFL Next Gen Stats tracking data, then adjusted by in-house risk/trading teams. Larger books (FanDuel, DraftKings) run proprietary models; smaller books often license market-maker pricing or copy the leaders. Because books post so many props and can't price each to the standard of the game lines, props are the "biggest, softest corner of the market" — game lines have decades of modeling and sharp enforcement, props do not.

NFL player props typically post early in the week (often Sunday night/Monday for the following week for marquee QBs, with the full board filling in as the week progresses). **Prop lines are usually softest when they first post, before the market corrects** — early value is a core edge.

**Why lines move:** (1) injury news — the biggest driver; a receiver's number moves more when the player ahead of him is ruled OUT than on his own practice report; QB news moves everything; O-line injuries move opponent sack props and team totals; (2) sharp action — syndicates with beat-writer access and models hammer a number; (3) public money — floods marquee sides late; (4) weather — sustained wind (not cold/rain) reliably suppresses passing and kicking. **Steam** in props is a sudden, uniform move across the whole market from smart money hitting many books at once; because props carry **low limits, a single sharp bet can move a prop several points within minutes**, so prop steam happens faster and on thinner action than game-line steam.

**Vig/hold benchmarks:** point spreads/totals hold ~4.5–4.8%; standard player props run ~8–15%; anytime-TD and other multi-outcome "field" markets run 6–10%+; 4+ leg SGPs and parlays routinely hold 20–30%+. In-game/live props run higher than pre-game (often 8–10% vs 4–5%).

### QUARTERBACK PROPS

**Passing yards (O/U + alternates).** The most liquid, most efficiently priced QB market. Alt lines run in ~5–25 yard increments. Drivers: implied team total, game total, pace (seconds/play), PROE (pass rate over expectation), opponent pass defense, pressure rate allowed by the O-line, and game script (trailing = more attempts). Settles on final official passing yards (OT included). Edge appears when a pace/PROE read suggests a shootout the posted total hasn't captured, or when the book lags O-line/slot-WR injury news. Line-shop: two books often post 246.5 vs 248.5 — take the better number and price.

**Passing attempts / completions.** Driven by pace, PROE, and game script far more than talent. Trailing teams throw more; run-heavy game scripts suppress both. Completions add a completion-percentage/aDOT layer (short, high-percentage passers clear completion lines more reliably; deep passers are higher variance). Both offered with alt lines and 1Q/1H variants on DraftKings.

**Passing touchdowns (0.5 / 1.5 / 2.5 / 3.5).** Almost always posted at 1.5, occasionally 2.5 for an elite arm or 0.5 for a backup/bad weather. This market's **entire hold lives in the juice** — all books post the identical 1.5 and simply charge different prices, so line-shopping is purely mechanical and skipping it donates edge. Note the base rate from a 2024–25 sample of 1,021 starts with 20+ pass attempts: even a starting NFL QB throws **zero** TD passes **21.1%** of the time — better than one start in five, including the best ones. Over 1.5 passing TDs hits only **45.9%** of the time (the Under is the majority side), making the fair price at league average roughly **+118** — so any book charging materially worse than that on the Over is overcharging. Drivers: red-zone efficiency, opponent red-zone TD rate allowed, implied team total, whether the team scores TDs vs FGs.

**Interceptions thrown.** Usually 0.5. High variance, near-random week to week. Drivers: opponent defense's INT/pressure rate, QB's turnover-worthy-play rate, game script (garbage-time chucking). A frequent trap as a straight bet; better as an SGP correlation leg (e.g., opponent win + QB Over INTs).

**Longest completion.** Settles Under if the QB records no completion. Driven by aDOT, deep-ball rate, and opponent's tendency to allow explosive plays. Boom-or-bust.

**Pass + rush yards (combined).** Useful for mobile QBs where rushing floor stabilizes the number. Settles on the sum of official passing and rushing yards.

**QB rushing yards / rushing attempts / anytime rushing TD.** Only relevant for mobile QBs (Jackson, Hurts, Murray, Daniels, Allen). QB rushing yards count toward QB rushing props only, not RB props. Drivers: designed-run rate, scramble rate, red-zone/goal-line QB run role (the "tush push" makes Hurts/Allen elite anytime-TD candidates), and pressure (a mobile QB vs a high-pressure defense may scramble more). QB anytime rushing TD counts only rushing scores — a passing TD never counts for a QB's anytime-TD prop.

**Sacks taken.** Driven by opponent pass-rush win rate, own O-line pass-block win rate/pressure allowed, QB time-to-throw and pressure-to-sack conversion rate, and pass volume. FanDuel treats a sack as a rushing attempt and an interception as an incomplete pass for offensive-stat settlement purposes.

**Passer rating props.** Rarely offered as standalone O/U on FD/DK weekly menus; more common as a novelty/specials or futures item. When offered, driven by the same inputs as yards + TD − INT efficiency.

**First QB to throw a TD / QB to throw a TD in each half.** Specials-style markets. "First to throw a TD" is a race driven by which offense scores TDs early (aggressive opening scripts, pace). "TD in each half" needs sustained scoring in both halves — correlated with a high team total and a competitive (non-blowout) script.

### RUNNING BACK PROPS

**Rushing yards (O/U + alts).** Core RB market. Volume first (carries, snap share), efficiency second (YPC, yards after contact, opponent run defense/DVOA, run-block win rate), then game script (leading teams run more late). Lines run ~35.5 for backups to 105.5+ for workhorses. Settles on official rushing yards. Kneel-downs count as negative rushing attempts/yards (rarely matters).

**Rushing attempts/carries (+ alts).** Highly script-dependent and among the most correlated legs with game total and spread — favorites in positive scripts get carries late. A strong SGP anchor.

**Longest rush.** Settles Under if no carry recorded. Boom-or-bust; driven by explosive-run rate and opponent's explosive-run-allowed rate.

**Rush + receiving yards (combined).** Stabilizes three-down backs (McCaffrey, Barkley-type) by combining floors. Reduces variance vs rushing-only.

**Receptions / receiving yards.** Pass-catching backs only. Driven by route participation, target share, and passing-down role. A back with 80% of carries plus the two-minute/passing-down role is a much stronger over than the box score implies.

**Anytime TD / first TD / last TD / 2+ TDs.** The most bet player prop in football. Per DataStreak's log of 1,761 games, **workhorse RBs (10+ carries) scored a TD in 49.6% of games — nearly a coin flip — while receivers with 5+ targets scored in just 34.4%.** That ~15-point gap is the structural truth: goal-line carries convert far better than perimeter targets. Drivers, in order: goal-line/red-zone role (who takes the carry at the 2), team implied total, game script (favorites run at the goal line). Settles only when the player physically possesses the ball in the end zone. 2+ TDs and 3+ TDs are higher-variance, higher-payout derivatives. First TD is a longshot dressed as a star pick — bet the goal-line role, not the name, and keep stakes small.

### RECEIVER / TIGHT END PROPS

**Receptions (+ alts).** Driven by target share, route participation (snaps run as a route, not just snaps), aDOT (low-aDOT slot receivers hit reception overs more reliably; deep threats are boom-or-bust), and QB style. One of the softer markets for WR2/WR3s the public ignores.

**Receiving yards (+ alts).** Volume (targets, air yards) × efficiency (aDOT, YAC, coverage matchup — does the player draw shadow coverage?). Public bets WR1s, leaving secondary options mispriced. A receiver can post 102 yards on 4 targets and still be a fragile weekly over — check the target floor.

**Longest reception.** Settles Under if no catch. Deep-threat market; high variance.

**Targets (where offered).** The purest "opportunity" market; less commonly posted but valuable when it is, because it strips out efficiency variance.

**TE-specific note.** Tight ends see fewer targets but a larger share inside the 20, so their receiving-yards and TD props swing hardest on scheme fit and red-zone role.

**Anytime TD / first / last / 2+ TDs.** Same mechanics as RBs. Red-zone target share (25%+ is the reliability threshold) and goal-line fade/slant role matter more than total yardage. TEs are efficient red-zone TD sources.

### DEFENSIVE & SPECIAL TEAMS PROPS

**This is the softest corner of the board — and the settlement rules are the trap.** Both books settle off the **official NFL gamebook**, which uses the LOWER official tackle count, not the higher team/coaches' film-charted number. Bettors modeling off PFF/charted data will overestimate.

**Tackles + Assists (combined).** Softest, most predictable, lowest-vig defensive prop. **Critical book difference: FanDuel's Tackles + Assists INCLUDES special-teams tackles; DraftKings EXCLUDES them** (defensive plays only) — this can flip a bet on a special-teams contributor. Analytical inputs, in order:
- **Snap share / every-down role** — the #1 driver. Only bet full-time, three-down defenders. A player who splits snaps (e.g., part edge, part off-ball LB) has a much lower and noisier forecast.
- **Position baselines** — elite three-down LBs run a ~16–19% "tackle rate" (share of team tackles); 13–15% is a solid full-timer. League-average LB tackles allowed is roughly 16.5 per game.
- **Opponent run/pass rate & PROE** — more opponent run plays = more tackle chances for box LBs/safeties. A run-heavy opponent is bullish; a pass-heavy opponent (65%+) is bearish for LB tackles.
- **Pace / expected total plays** — more plays = more opportunity.
- **Game script** — trailing opponents pass more (fewer box tackles); leading opponents run to bleed clock (more).
- **Missed-tackle rate / tackles-over-expected** — refines the projection.

**Solo tackles.** Separate, lower line than combined. Same inputs, but noisier because the solo/assist split is scorer-dependent.

**Sacks (player).** Half-sacks count as 0.5; on an anytime-sack (0.5) market a half-sack settles "Yes." Most edges priced O/U 0.5; 1.5 for elite in plus matchups; 2.5 is rare and Under-juiced (team average is only ~2.5 sacks per game). Inputs: pass-rush win rate (elite ~30%+, meaning the rusher still LOSES ~70% of reps), opponent pass-block win rate/pressure allowed, opponent O-line injuries, opponent QB time-to-throw and pressure-to-sack conversion, pass volume, and negative game script (trailing opponent in long-yardage). **Even elite rushers post frequent zero-sack games** — sacks are low-frequency and require winning the rep AND the QB holding the ball; treat as high-variance, situational parlay legs only.

**Interceptions (defensive) / passes defended.** Near-random; INTs are among the least predictable stats. Passes defended is more volume-stable (tied to targets at the defender). Best as longshot/SGP legs, not core bets.

**Defensive/special-teams TD.** Offered as a player and game-level yes/no. Extremely high variance; a defensive/ST TD scorer falls under "Any Other" team grouping in scorer markets.

**Kicking props (in depth).**
- **Kicking points** = 3 × FG made + 1 × PAT made. Model it as `3 × E[FGM] + 1 × E[PATM]`, built from the team's implied points, pace, and TD-vs-FG split. If a kicker is active but plays no snap, kicking-points bets stand (FanDuel rule).
- **Field goals made (+ alts).** Driven by (1) FG-attempt volume — a function of scoring and how often the offense stalls in FG range; (2) attempt distance distribution (every 10 yards past 40 cuts make% by ~5–8%); (3) weather — **wind is the primary variable** (a 50-yarder hits 80%+ in a dome but drops to ~60–65% in 15+ mph wind); (4) kicker make%; (5) opponent defense — paradoxically, a strong defense that forces FGs instead of TDs BOOSTS kicker props.
- **Longest FG made.** Distance/leg-strength and dome-vs-outdoor dependent.
- **Extra points made.** A pure function of TDs scored + kick vs 2-point tendencies. Two-point conversions don't credit the kicker.
- Kickers in domes are far more reliable; always check wind before betting outdoor kicker props.

**Punting props (punts, punting yards).** Offered where available. Counterintuitively correlated with a BAD offense (more three-and-outs = more punts). Driven by offensive efficiency, pace, and game script.

### TEAM & GAME-LEVEL MARKETS

- **Moneyline, spread, total** — the sharpest, most efficient markets (~4.5% hold). Shop the number (a half-point around 3 and 7 is huge) then the price.
- **Alternate spreads/totals** — FanDuel offers more alt spreads/totals than DraftKings. Useful for buying past key numbers or building SGPs.
- **Team totals** — isolate one team's points; cleaner for pace/PROE reads than the game total.
- **First half / second half / quarter lines & totals** — derivative markets; OT is excluded from these segment markets (it counts only for full-game). Sharp opportunity when you have a strong read on how a team starts or finishes. Books price 1Q/1H player and team props with less precision.
- **Race to X points (10/15/20/30)** — about game FLOW, not final score. Prioritize opening-drive/first-possession efficiency, early tempo, and 1Q scoring rates over season averages. "Neither" is a live option for low-scoring reads.
- **First/last team to score, first scoring play type (TD/FG/safety), first score method & team, time of first TD/first score** — driven by pace, opening-script aggressiveness, and red-zone efficiency. A 6-point TD, 3-point FG, and 2-point safety count as scoring plays; 1- and 2-point conversions do NOT.
- **Highest-scoring half/quarter; both teams to score in every quarter; both teams to score 20+** — parlay-flavored game props; correlate with high totals and competitive scripts.
- **Winning margin / exact score bands, total TDs, total FGs, total sacks, total INTs/turnovers in game** — derivative counting markets; higher hold.
- **Will there be OT / a safety / a defensive or ST TD / a successful 2-point conversion** — novelty yes/no props, all longshots with meaningful hold; safeties and 2-point conversions are rare-event Unders.
- **Shortest/longest TD, longest FG in game** — distributional novelty props.
- **Team to score first AND win; any player to score 2+ TDs** — parlay-builder correlation markets.

If a game is abandoned after starting, already-settled markets stand; if a TD has been scored, first-TD-scorer stands but last-TD-scorer is voided. Markets are void if a game is postponed unless the outcome is already unequivocally determined.

### SPECIALTY & NOVELTY MARKETS

**Same Game Parlay & SGP+.** FanDuel pioneered the SGP builder (fast, intuitive, shows individual leg odds; green/blue-yellow "SGP" tag marks eligible games). DraftKings' builder shows only the full parlay price, not individual leg odds (workaround: build frontline bets as a normal parlay). **SGP+** (both books) combines multiple SGPs from different games into one ticket. Both apply a "correlation tax" — heavily correlated "everything good happens for one team" builds pay far less than naive multiplication because the book already priced the correlation. **The invisible edge: compare the SGP price to the fair joint probability.** Because the two books price correlation differently, the same build can be +290 on FanDuel and +180 on DraftKings — always shop SGPs across books. Highest value is in 2–3 leg builds; 5+ leg SGPs carry 15–20%+ built-in hold. Slightly contrarian builds (a team to win + an under; a star over + opposing team to cover) often beat the correlation tax. Live/in-game SGP and SGP+ are available during games on both books.

**Book-specific menus.** DraftKings: largest catalog (70–90 markets/game, 20–30 per star incl. alts down to 0.5-yard increments), Pick6 flat-payout prop builder, head-to-head player matchups, softer niche pricing, and the exclusive **Official Sportsbook and Odds Provider of ESPN effective December 1, 2025** (announced Nov 6, 2025, replacing the terminated ESPN Bet/Penn deal), but aggressive limits on winners. FanDuel: more alt spreads/totals, most liquid anytime-TD market (moves within seconds of injury news, pricing a tick better on starting RBs/WR1s), slicker app, distinctive first-team-to-X and novelty props. Both launched prediction-market products (DraftKings Predictions, FanDuel Predicts) in late 2025.

**Player milestone markets.** 100+ rushing yards, 300+ passing yards, 100+ receiving yards, 2+/3+ TDs — these are just alt lines expressed as milestones; price them as such and shop the number.

**Touchdown-scorer combos & "record a sack AND team wins"–style markets.** Pre-built correlated parlays; convenient but check the price against the fair joint number — the book has already taxed the correlation.

**Live/in-game markets.** Next play type, next drive result, live spreads/totals/props and live SGP. Higher vig (8–10%), fast-moving; edge windows appear on in-game injuries and scheme shifts (a team abandoning the run) before the book re-prices.

**Futures relevant to props.** MVP, OPOY, DPOY, ORoY/DRoY, season-long passing/rushing/receiving-yardage leaders, team win totals, division winners, conference, Super Bowl. Softest pre-season when uncertainty is highest. Voter-bias notes: MVP goes almost exclusively to QBs — the last non-QB winner was Adrian Peterson (Minnesota, 2012 season), who took 30½ of 50 votes over Peyton Manning and was himself the first RB winner since LaDainian Tomlinson in 2006; QBs have won every year since. OPOY increasingly rewards skill-position players having monster years; DPOY skews to sack leaders, elite pass rushers, or dominant DBs. Season-long yardage-leader and player-total futures let you lock value before Week 1 line shifts.

### Correlation notes for parlay building
- **Positively correlated (books tax these):** QB passing yards + his WR's receiving yards; RB rushing yards + RB anytime TD in a blowout script; team total over + its QB passing TD over; favorite ML + its RB rushing attempts over.
- **Use contrarian/mildly-correlated builds** to beat the tax: team to win + game under; star over + opposing team to cover.
- **Avoid** stacking a QB's passing yards with his own WR when the price offers no real value — it adds correlated risk, not edge. **Skip safety tackle + INT bundles for parlays** (INT randomness sinks the ticket); save them for straight bets.

### Tools for shopping props & finding +EV
- **OddsJam** — scans 50–150+ books, flags +EV by comparing retail prices to a sharp-book (Pinnacle/Circa/Bookmaker) no-vig consensus; arbitrage, middles, bet tracking, CLV. ~$99–199/mo tiers; free tier is read-only odds comparison.
- **Unabated** — sharp-book-based fair values (the "Unabated Line"), player-props and futures simulators (10,000-run sims), alt-line and partial-game-derivative calculators, middle pricing. Built for experienced bettors; steeper learning curve (~$199/mo).
- **Outlier (Outlier.bet)** — prop-focused; +EV wagers, line movement, injury alerts, deep player-prop and DFS (PrizePicks/Underdog) tooling; more affordable Pro tier.
- **OddsShopper (OS Pro)** — de-vigged true odds, SGP correlation pricing, Tails marketplace.
- **PropsBot.AI, BettingPros, RotoWire props tool, PropsCash, Props Optimizer** — projection- and edge-score-based prop finders across FD/DK/Caesars/BetMGM/PrizePicks/Underdog.
- **RotoWire / DataStreak / Sharp Football Analysis / nfelo / FantasyPros snap counts / NFL Next Gen Stats** — for the underlying inputs (PROE, pace, pass-block/pass-rush win rate, snap share, target share, tackle rates).

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Foundation (do this first).** Open and fund BOTH FanDuel and DraftKings; sharps use both to shop every prop. Read each book's house rules for your state before betting (settlement rules decide bets after the game). Internalize the gamebook-vs-charting rule for defensive props and the FanDuel-includes/DraftKings-excludes special-teams-tackles divergence.

**Stage 2 — Attack the soft markets.** Prioritize, in order: (1) **every-down LB Tackles + Assists** — bet only confirmed three-down players, weight opponent run rate/PROE, pace, and game script, and model off the OFFICIAL gamebook baseline (not PFF); (2) **WR2/WR3 receiving yards & receptions** the public ignores; (3) **alt lines that lag injury news** (O-line, slot WR, backfield). Bet early in the week when lines are softest.

**Stage 3 — Line-shop and quantify.** Subscribe to one +EV/odds tool (OddsJam or OddsShopper for breadth; Outlier or Unabated for prop depth). Never bet a passing-TD or anytime-TD prop without shopping — the hold lives in the juice (recall the Over 1.5 passing-TD fair price is ~+118). For SGPs, always compare the price across both books to the fair joint number and stay at 2–3 legs.

**Stage 4 — Kicking and situational.** Build a kicking-points model (`3×E[FGM] + 1×E[PATM]`), and always check wind on outdoor games. Use sack and defensive-INT props only as small, situational SGP legs given their variance.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** If a book starts limiting your prop stakes to token amounts (a sign you're beating them), diversify to more books and lean on alt lines and SGPs where limits are looser. If your closing-line value (CLV) is consistently negative, you're beating yourself on stale reads — bet later or tighten your model. If a prop's hold exceeds ~10% and you can't find a sharper price, pass.

**Avoid (highest hold / worst EV):** 4+ leg SGPs and same-game parlays (20–30%+ hold); first-TD scorer and exotic scorer combos as anything but small lottery tickets; live props unless you have a specific in-game read; INT/turnover and safety/2-point novelty yes/no props as core bets.

## Caveats
- **Settlement rules vary by state and change.** The FanDuel/DraftKings specifics here (special-teams tackles, half-sacks, OT treatment, kicking-points-if-no-snap) are drawn from published house rules and reputable secondary summaries, but you must read the current version for your exact wager and location. The DraftKings "special teams excluded from Tackles + Assists" point comes from a secondary summary and should be verified against DK's primary NFL rules; explicit house-rule text enumerating "passes defended" settlement was not locatable and should be confirmed live.
- **OT treatment is nuanced:** full-game player props include overtime (they settle on final gamebook totals); quarter/half segment props exclude it. Confirm on the specific market.
- **Book-comparison claims** (which book is "softer," alt-line counts, limits) reflect 2025–26 reporting from betting-media and tool sites; menus, limits, and pricing shift constantly and are account-specific. Books do not publish per-market dollar limits; defensive props simply carry among the lowest limits on the board.
- **Hold/EV figures are ranges,** not audited per-market constants; several come from betting-content sites and self-reported tool data — treat as directional.
- **Base-rate stats** (e.g., 49.6% workhorse-RB TD rate, 34.4% 5+-target-WR TD rate, 21.1% of QB starts with zero passing TDs, Over 1.5 passing TDs hitting 45.9%) come from specific multi-season samples cited by analytics sites and are illustrative, not guarantees.
