# How Sportsbooks and Fantasy Sites Build Betting Lines and Player Props

## TL;DR
- **The numbers you see are machine-generated, not guessed.** Modern sportsbooks buy exclusive official league data (the NFL through Genius Sports; NBA/NHL/MLB primarily through Sportradar), feed it into projection and Monte Carlo simulation engines, and convert probability distributions into American odds with a built-in margin ("vig"). Player props for yardage use full statistical distributions (Poisson/negative-binomial/simulation), while touchdown props use Poisson/binomial goal-line models.
- **Books "win most of these bets" mainly through margin and product mix, not superior prediction.** A standard side/total holds ~4.8%; individual NFL player props typically hold ~6–10%; and same-game parlays (SGPs) — the most-promoted product — hold 20–35%+. Blended U.S. hold rose from ~7% (2019) to ~10%+ (2025) largely because books steered bettors into props and SGPs.
- **The public's realistic edge is line-shopping, beating the closing line (CLV), and exploiting slow-moving/soft prop markets — but winners get limited or banned.** DFS pick'em apps (PrizePicks, Underdog) shade lines off sportsbook consensus with fixed payouts that embed a per-pick house edge; season-long fantasy projections (ESPN, Yahoo) are a separate product built from usage-share regression, not betting markets.

## Key Findings

1. **Data is an exclusive, league-controlled commodity.** The NFL's official real-time play-by-play, Next Gen Stats tracking data, and low-latency "Watch and Bet" video feed run exclusively through Genius Sports (deal originally signed 2021, extended through the end of the 2029 NFL season). NBA, NHL, and MLB (internationally) official data run primarily through Sportradar. Genius says it powers "over 98% of the legalized U.S. sports betting market with official NFL data."

2. **Most lines are made by a handful of B2B suppliers plus a few in-house trading giants.** Kambi, Sportradar, Genius Sports (BetVision/betting tech), Huddle, and the former SimpleBet supply pricing to operators. The market leaders built in-house: DraftKings acquired SBTech (2020), Sports IQ Analytics (2024, props), and SimpleBet (2024, micro-betting); FanDuel prices in-house via Flutter's global "Flutter Edge" trading operation and OpenBet engine.

3. **Pricing has shifted from human oddsmakers to algorithmic/AI trading at scale.** Kambi launched AI-driven autonomous trading ahead of the 2022 World Cup; Genius Sports and Huddle run Monte Carlo simulation engines (millions of simulations) that price props, micro-markets, and correlated SGP legs simultaneously.

4. **The math is distribution-based, not point-estimate based.** A game spread + total is converted into implied team totals, then into plays/drives/usage; player outcomes are simulated as distributions. Posted yardage lines sit near the median (not mean) of skewed distributions, and touchdown/anytime-TD props use Poisson/binomial models driven by red-zone usage.

5. **Correlation is the engine of SGP profitability.** Because outcomes in one game are correlated, books use Monte Carlo/copula methods to price the true (higher) joint probability — then still shade the payout, producing 20–35%+ holds.

6. **Retail books copy market-maker prices and shade toward public bias; market-makers (Pinnacle, Circa) welcome sharps.** The public's durable tools are devigging sharp lines, line shopping, and tracking CLV.

## Details

### 1. Data Sources — Where the raw numbers come from

**The official data rights ecosystem.** Since PASPA was struck down in 2018, U.S. leagues have monetized data through exclusive distribution deals:

- **NFL → Genius Sports.** First partnered 2021 in a deal reported at ~US$120 million per season that also gave the NFL equity in Genius; extended in 2023 through the 2027-28 season, then again in a June 11, 2025 agreement that runs "through the end of the 2029 NFL season" (Genius Sports SEC Form 6-K). As part of that extension Genius issued the NFL 9,500,000 warrants at a $0.01 exercise price (4.5M vested immediately, 5M vesting April 1, 2028). Genius is the "exclusive distributor of real-time, official play-by-play statistics, proprietary Next Gen Stats ('NGS') data and the League's official sports betting data feed," plus the sole supplier of the low-latency "Watch and Bet" / BetVision live-video feeds to sportsbooks. Per NFL.com: "Today, Genius Sports powers over 98% of the legalized U.S. sports betting market with official NFL data, driving innovations such as player props, micro-betting, same-game parlays and personalized data-driven advertising content." Genius beat prior partner Sportradar for the NFL rights.
- **NBA → Sportradar (exclusive worldwide, 2023-24 through 2030-31).** Sportico's reporting of the regulatory filing valued the cash-plus-equity package at "more than $1 billion," while Sports Business Journal reported "more than $3 billion" over eight years. The NBA receives a ~3% stake via warrants with a "$0.01 strike price and an eight-year vesting schedule, 20% vesting up front," and the deal also covers the WNBA and NBA G League. (The NBA had a prior non-exclusive arrangement with both Sportradar and Genius from 2018.)
- **NHL → Sportradar** (10-year global deal announced 2021; Sportradar had been the NHL's global distributor since 2015), including real-time data from the NHL's Puck and Player Tracking technology and betting-streaming rights.
- **MLB → Sportradar** internationally (from 2019); Genius Sports also has an MLB deal.

**Official vs. unofficial data, and why latency matters.** Official league data is the fastest, most authoritative feed; leagues and their partners lobby to require its use for in-play settlement (Illinois and Tennessee are cited as states requiring official league data to settle live in-game bets). For live/in-play and micro-betting, latency is the whole game: standard HTTP streaming (10–30 seconds) is unusable for live wagering; the practical working range for sportsbook streams is ~2–8 seconds, with sub-second to ~2s for micro-betting. Crucially, books keep the **video slightly behind the data feed** so the odds are never "spoiled" — the bettor watching the stream is intentionally a beat behind the trader's data. Low latency also "reduces the window for latency arbitrage" (savvy bettors exploiting stale odds). Micro-betting (next pitch/next play) demands single-digit-millisecond pricing response.

**Other feeds.** Beyond play-by-play, pricing engines ingest tracking data (NFL Next Gen Stats; NBA optical tracking via Second Spectrum — routes, separation, touch time, speed/positioning), injury/inactive reports, confirmed lineups, and weather. The edge over the public is partly speed (official feeds arrive before box scores or TV) and partly integration (all feeds flowing into one automated pricing engine).

### 2. Who actually makes the lines

**B2B suppliers vs. in-house trading.** Two models coexist:
- **B2B odds/trading suppliers** price markets for operators that don't build their own. **Kambi** has priced markets and managed risk for ~20 years, runs on 70+ operators, and offers a "Turnkey Sportsbook" (full trading/risk/front-end) plus "Odds Feed+" (odds via a single API). Notably, Kambi supplies neither DraftKings nor FanDuel today. **Sportradar** offers managed trading services (MTS). **Genius Sports** provides betting technology/BetVision and Monte Carlo-based pricing. **Huddle** and the former **SimpleBet** specialize in micro-market/SGP pricing.
- **In-house trading** at the market leaders:
  - **DraftKings** initially used Kambi (from June 2018). It then acquired **SBTech** via a three-way combination with Diamond Eagle Acquisition Corp. (announced Dec 2019, closed April 23, 2020 at an ~$3.3 billion equity market cap), becoming what DraftKings called "the only vertically integrated pure-play sports betting and online gaming company based in the United States" with control of its own trading platform; the Kambi deal was wound down by Sept 30, 2021. It has since bolted on **Sports IQ Analytics** (2024, AI/ML player-props pricing; Sports IQ plus Dijon Systems listed at ~$28.2M cash plus equity and up to $33.3M contingent per its 10-Q) and **SimpleBet** (2024, micro-betting; reported up to ~$195M — $70M upfront plus up to $125M earnouts per JMP Securities).
  - **FanDuel** prices online in-house through parent **Flutter Entertainment**. (Important correction to a common framing: IGT's role for FanDuel is the *retail* sportsbook platform — kiosks, PAM — renewed through 2028; it is *not* FanDuel's online pricing.) FanDuel's online odds were built first by its own team, then on Paddy Power Betfair/Flutter's global platform (OpenBet engine), leveraging what Flutter brands the **"Flutter Edge"** — a shared global trading operation of ~100 traders from Dublin to Melbourne pricing FanDuel, Paddy Power, Betfair, Sportsbet, Sisal and more. Sportradar is FanDuel's *data* supplier for NBA/MLB/NHL. FanDuel's own Risk & Trading team ("the brains behind the odds") uses "stats, in-house models, industry news and our opinions to help formulate the odds."
  - **Bet365** is known for a strong in-house model.

**The shift to automated/algorithmic pricing.** Kambi's third-generation AI trading (launched ahead of the 2022 World Cup) prices and trades "without human intervention," delivering "almost instantaneous publishing times and a far greater depth of markets"; Kambi CEO Werner Becher reported that 60% of Q1 2026 network bets were AI-traded (up from 49% in 2025). Kambi's SVP Analytics is David Jacquet. Genius Sports (Thomas Holland, VP Product) describes Monte Carlo models as "the hidden engine behind betting's next revolution." The practical result: the hundreds of props per game are machine-priced at scale; humans supervise and intervene on outliers.

### 3. The modeling — how prop lines are actually derived

**Step 1: From game line to team/game structure.** The market spread and total are the anchor. Implied team total = (game total ± spread) / 2. Example: a 3-point favorite in a 50-point game has an implied total of 26.5. Books convert these expected points into expected plays, pace, drive counts, and scoring opportunities — the scaffolding for individual projections.

**Step 2: Usage/market-share inputs.** Player projections run on usage: target share, snap share, air yards, route participation, and rush-attempt share, adjusted for the opposing defense's tendencies against that stat and position, and for game script (favorites run more; underdogs pass more when trailing).

**Step 3: Distribution modeling, not a single number.** A yardage projection is a curve, not a point. Because yardage is right-skewed and over-dispersed (variance exceeds the mean), books use distributions such as **negative binomial** (a gamma-Poisson mixture that handles over-dispersion), gamma, or log-normal shapes, or full **Monte Carlo simulation** of the game play-by-play. Critically, for a skewed distribution the posted line is placed near the **median** (the 50/50 point) rather than the mean, so the over/under is a genuine coin flip before vig. This is why a receiving line at 62.5 vs. 68.5 flips a bet from play to pass.

**Step 4: Touchdown and anytime-TD props.** Scoring counts are modeled with **Poisson/binomial** processes. Passing TDs per start average ~1.5 and are "almost perfectly Poisson," so Over 1.5 hits ~45.9% at baseline (fair ~+118) — and because the half-point line can only sit at 1.5 or 2.5, the book adjusts the *juice* rather than the line. For anytime-TD, the model assigns each roster a share of the team's expected TDs based on red-zone target share, goal-line carry share, and expected touches, then converts each player's scoring probability to American odds. Since ~86.6% of rushing TDs since 2010 came from inside the red zone (74% inside the 10, 57.4% inside the 5), goal-line role dominates. Note that anytime-TD is a one-sided "Yes" market: the sum of all players' implied probabilities on the board can far exceed 100% (a much larger built-in margin than a two-way 104-105% moneyline). Most starters price +200 to +400; workhorse goal-line backs as short as +110, low-volume role players +900 and up.

**Step 5: Correlation and simulation for SGPs.** Outcomes within a game are not independent (QB over 300 yards → team more likely to win; game over → multiple players over). Books run **full-game Monte Carlo simulators** — Genius describes pricing "driven by millions of simulations, where every possible bet combination is considered, including the relationship between legs," and Huddle documented using an OLAP database (DuckDB) to encode 10,000–20,000 simulated game outcomes for fast SGP retrieval. Positive correlation raises the true joint probability, so a fairly priced SGP pays *less* than a naive independent parlay; negative correlation pays more (e.g., FanDuel's +472 vs. a naive +264 on a negatively correlated pair). FanDuel launched SGPs in 2019; rivals copied in 2020-21. On Super Bowl LIX, Caesars reported SGP bets outnumbered single wagers.

**Step 6: Converting probability to odds and setting the vig.** The model's fair probability is converted to a price, then margin is added. On a two-way prop, -110/-110 implies 52.4% each (104.8% total) — a 4.8% hold. Books set **asymmetric juice** (e.g., Over -115 / Under -105, or +105/-125) when the model favors one side, when they expect lopsided public action (shading the popular "over" or star side), or to manage risk. Half-point placement (e.g., 249.5) is chosen to avoid pushes and to sit at the median probability.

### 4. The business/edge side — why books win

**Hold by bet type (the core reason books win).**
- **Sides/totals:** ~4.5–5% (4.76% at -110/-110).
- **Individual NFL player props:** typically ~6–10% — the Wizard of Odds categorizes major star props at 4–6%, secondary props 6–10%, exotic/specialty props 10–20%, and live props 8–15%. A -115/-115 prop holds ~6.5%; -120/-120 holds ~9%. A worked example: passing yards Over -115 / Under -105 = 53.5% + 51.2% = 104.7% hold ("a reasonable hold for a major quarterback prop"). A three-way rebound prop (Over/Exactly/Under) can hold ~19%. Books know prop bettors are less price-sensitive.
- **Parlays/SGPs:** 20–35%+. Nevada books have held ~30–31% on parlays since 1984 vs. ~5-6% on other bets. Deutsche Bank's illustrative SGP math produced a ~21% expected hold on one example ticket. SGPs are estimated at 35–40% of gross gaming revenue at major operators (up from under 20% in 2021), and per FanDuel/Flutter, parlays make up ~70% of the brand's NBA and NFL betting. Blended U.S. hold rose from ~7.0% (2019) to ~10%+ (2025) per AGA figures — several billion dollars a year in extra retained revenue.

**Vig mechanics and devigging.** "Fair"/no-vig odds are computed by removing the overround. Four standard **devig methods**: **multiplicative** (proportional, the default; best for balanced two-way markets), **additive** (equal margin per outcome; shifts probability toward longshots), **power** (raises probabilities to a constant exponent), and **Shin** (iterative model that accounts for informed/insider money and corrects favorite-longshot bias). Method choice barely matters on balanced markets but diverges sharply on lopsided lines; for high-vig retail props, power or Shin is preferred. Sharps devig a sharp book (Pinnacle) to get fair probabilities, then compare to soft retail prices.

**Balanced book vs. position-taking.** The textbook model is balancing action to collect the vig risk-free. The modern reality is that books frequently **take positions** — they price to their own models, accept imbalance against recreational money, and use sharp action as information. Retail books depend on recreational losses to cover the vig.

**Risk management, limits, and bans.** Books limit bet sizes on props (thinner markets), move lines in response to sharp money and "steam," and restrict or ban winning players. At the Sept. 11, 2024 Massachusetts Gaming Commission roundtable, BetMGM senior director of compliance Sarah Brennan said "BetMGM limits approximately 1% of Massachusetts patrons presently"; FanDuel VP Cory Fox said only 0.043% of FanDuel's 2023 Massachusetts wagers were placed at the player's maximum allowed amount, and the MGC's own analysis (chair Jordan Maynard, via StateHouse News) found just 0.64% of Massachusetts accounts limited as of December 2024. In practice sharp winners on soft books are cut hard (widely reported max bets slashed to a few dollars), often "in blocks" by market (e.g., limited on NBA props but not MLB).

**Market-makers vs. retail books.** **Pinnacle** (margins ~2–3%, high limits, "winners welcome," uses sharp action to sharpen prices; not available in the U.S. since 2007), **Circa**, and **BetOnline** are market-makers. Retail books (**FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, Caesars**) build lines off the market/other books, shade toward public bias (popular players, overs, favorites, star QBs), advertise heavily, and limit winners. Peer-to-peer exchanges (e.g., Novig) run lower holdback (~102–104%) and don't limit, but have thinner prop liquidity.

**Promotions.** Profit boosts and bonus bets are priced as marketing spend calibrated against expected value; they lower — but usually don't eliminate — the house edge, and are concentrated in high-margin products (parlays/SGPs) to steer behavior.

### 5. Where the public can find edge

- **Closing Line Value (CLV).** The closing line is the sharpest, most efficient price. Consistently beating it is the strongest predictor of long-term profit — more reliable than short-run win rate. Books themselves use CLV to flag sharps. Prop markets are less efficient than sides/totals, so CLV swings (and edges) are larger there.
- **Prop market inefficiency.** Props have less historical data, less sharp attention, and less refined models than mainlines — the softest large market. Edge comes from doing more work than the market on usage, and from **line shopping** across books (the same anytime-TD can be -130 at one book and +100 at another; the same yardage line can move a bet to a pass).
- **Limits of public projection systems.** Public models and "AI pick" sites are frequently well-priced already; obvious matchups, hot/cold streaks, and ESPN storylines are priced in. Realistic long-run edges are a few points of ROI, not the 60%+ win rates marketed on social media.
- **The reality of limits/bans.** The structural catch: to the extent a bettor is genuinely good, retail books limit or ban them, pushing serious players to market-makers (where lines are already sharp), exchanges, or Circa.

### 6. DFS / fantasy sites

**DFS pick'em (PrizePicks, Underdog, Sleeper, ParlayPlay, Boom).** These are "more/less" products, legally structured as fantasy contests rather than sportsbooks. Key differences from a traditional book:
- **Fixed payouts by number of picks**, not per-side American odds. PrizePicks Power Play (all-or-nothing) pays roughly 3x/5x/10x/25x for 2/3/4/5 correct; Flex Play (PrizePicks) / Insured (Underdog) pays reduced amounts if you miss one. Underdog often pays slightly more at key tiers (e.g., 3-pick 6.5x vs. PrizePicks 6x; 2-pick 3.5x vs. 3x).
- **Embedded per-leg house edge.** Converting the payout table to a per-leg price, PrizePicks legs price around -119 (needing ~54.3% per leg to break even) while an Underdog flex structure can equate to ~-107 (~51.7%). Both are worse for the player than devigged sportsbook prices on the same stat, because the payout table *is* the price.
- **Lines shaded off sportsbook consensus.** Pick'em lines are largely derived from sportsbook prop consensus, then shaded; Underdog is generally sharper/tighter, PrizePicks softer on niche/tier-2 markets where edge lingers. PrizePicks tightens marquee/primetime lines fast; softer lines survive on low-attention markets.
- **Correlation restrictions and adjusted-payout picks.** To protect against correlated stacking, these apps restrict combinations (require multiple teams, cap same-player usage) and use adjusted-payout squares (PrizePicks "Demon"/"Goblin"; Underdog per-pick difficulty multipliers 0.7x–1.5x). Projections lock at submission even if the line later moves. Sharp users report line adjustments after their picks and account limitations.

**Season-long fantasy projections (ESPN, Yahoo, Sleeper) — a distinct product.** These predict *fantasy points*, not betting lines, and are not shaded for margin. Methodology: start from historical player data, assign predicted usage shares (dropbacks, carries, targets), then refine with statistical regression, efficiency metrics (yards/touch, red-zone conversion), pace/pass-rate, and context (coaching, injuries, age curves, weather). Yahoo blends multiple third-party providers (RotoWire, The BLITZ, FTN); ESPN uses a structured usage-share-then-regression process. Accuracy is modest — one analysis found Yahoo's weekly projections land within 10% of actual only ~20% of the time. The user's phrase "fantasy sites" likely conflates these projection products with DFS pick'em betting lines; they are related (both usage-driven) but serve different purposes.

## Recommendations

**For understanding/analysis:**
1. **Treat the posted line as a devigged probability, not a prediction.** Convert both sides to implied probability, strip the vig (multiplicative for balanced props; power/Shin for lopsided ones), and compare to your own estimate. Only the gap beyond the vig is potential edge.
2. **Use a sharp book (Pinnacle/Circa) as your "true price" reference** and treat retail prop lines as the soft prices to shop against.
3. **Track CLV over 100+ bets.** If you consistently beat the closing line, your process has edge regardless of short-run results; if you don't, it doesn't. This is the single most reliable self-diagnostic.

**For anyone betting:**
4. **Avoid SGPs and long parlays as a profit strategy** — 20–35%+ holds make them the worst EV on the board; they are entertainment, not investment.
5. **Line-shop every prop across multiple books**; half-point and juice differences are frequently decisive. On DFS pick'em, prefer the app with the better payout table for your entry size (often Underdog on small entries) and hunt soft niche-market lines.
6. **Expect to be limited if you win.** Plan around market-makers and exchanges rather than assuming durable access to soft books.

**Thresholds that change the approach:** If your realized CLV is negative across a large sample, stop — you're paying above-market prices. If a prop market is efficient (tight two-way pricing, sharp books present), treat it like a mainline and demand a real modeled edge; if it's soft (exotic prop, tier-2 market, secondary player), that's where the inefficiency lives.

## Caveats

- **Books keep exact methods proprietary.** The specific distributions, correlation matrices, and simulation parameters used by FanDuel, DraftKings, Kambi, Genius, and Huddle are not public. The distribution families and Monte Carlo approaches described here are documented by suppliers (Genius, Huddle), academic/quant sources, and industry explainers, but the precise in-house implementations are inferred, not disclosed.
- **Hold figures are estimates that vary by book, market, player, and week.** No audited regulatory report isolates a single "NFL player-prop hold %." The strongest transparent source is the Wizard of Odds prop-hold table; other prop-hold numbers come from betting-tool/affiliate sites and should be read as directionally consistent industry estimates. SGP/parlay hold ranges (20–35%) are well corroborated across trade coverage, AGA figures, and Nevada historical data.
- **Deal terms are often undisclosed or estimated.** The ~$120M/season NFL-Genius figure is reported by trade press (SportsPro); the NBA-Sportradar package was reported at "more than $1 billion" (Sportico) and "more than $3 billion" (Sports Business Journal), reflecting different valuation methods rather than a confirmed single figure.
- **Correction incorporated:** Contrary to a common framing, IGT never supplied FanDuel's core online trading — it supplies FanDuel's *retail* platform. FanDuel's online pricing is in-house via Flutter.
- **Some sources are speculative or marketing-driven.** Affiliate sites and AI-pick vendors have incentives to overstate available edge; league/supplier press releases have promotional spin. Numbers from those sources are flagged accordingly and cross-checked where possible.
- **Dates:** Current as of September 2026; data-rights deals and corporate ownership (e.g., prediction-market moves by DraftKings/Flutter in 2025–26) are evolving.
